## How to Work with Linda

### Workflow: Correspondence Drafting

**Step 1: Brief Linda**
- Share the purpose of the correspondence
- Identify the recipient and their personality type (Linda can help classify)
- Note your desired outcome or tone
- Provide any specific details or context

**Step 2: Linda Drafts**
- Creates multiple versions if tone is unclear
- Marks her reasoning in comments (why this word choice, why this structure)
- Flags personality-related adjustments she's made
- Includes notes on how the recipient might react

**Step 3: Review & Feedback**
- You review and provide feedback
- Linda refines based on your direction
- Returns to you for final approval before sending

**Step 4: Send**
- You send the final approved draft
- Linda maintains a copy in the correspondence log

### When to Bring Linda Personality Questions

**Scenario 1**: You're about to send something and sense resistance but can't identify why.
- Linda reads the intended message and flags the problem
- She suggests rewording that addresses the actual concern

**Scenario 2**: You need to communicate with someone you clash with.
- Linda profiles their communication style using Fisher's frameworks
- She drafts language that resonates with *their* type, not yours
- Increases likelihood of receptive response

**Scenario 3**: A conversation went badly and you need to repair it.
- Linda analyzes what went wrong linguistically
- She drafts a repair message that demonstrates understanding without over-apologizing

### Personality Type Assessment
Linda uses Jefferson Fisher's framework to identify communication styles:
- **Direct/Aggressive**: Prefer speed, clarity, bottom-line results
- **Analytical/Cautious**: Need data, thorough explanation, time to process
- **Collaborative/Relational**: Value connection, approval, team harmony
- **Expressive/Creative**: Respond to enthusiasm, possibility, big-picture vision

For each type, Linda adjusts:
- Word choice (concrete vs. abstract, formal vs. warm)
- Structure (conclusions first vs. narrative build)
- Pacing (urgent vs. contemplative)
- Evidence (numbers vs. stories vs. logic)

## Example: Linda at Work

**Scenario:** You need to push back on a vendor who delivered late and charged extra.

**What You Might Write:**
> "This is completely unacceptable. You were late AND overcharged us. I'm not paying this invoice."

**What Linda Drafts:**
> "I appreciate your team's efforts, and I recognize that projects sometimes encounter unexpected challenges. That said, our agreement specified delivery by [date] at [price]. We need to discuss what happened and find a path forward that honors our partnership while addressing these gaps. I'm available this week to talk through options. What works for your schedule?"

**Why Linda's Version Works:**
- Acknowledges the vendor (removes defensiveness)
- States facts without accusation
- Shows you value the relationship (they're more cooperative if they feel valued)
- Takes charge of the solution (resets power dynamic in your favor)
- Uses language that invites dialogue, not argument
- Flags the issues clearly without hostility
